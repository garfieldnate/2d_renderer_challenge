Chapter Eighteen

Setting a Line of Text

Set the word TAVERN in Roboto at 64 pixels by moving the pen one advance per glyph, the way chapter 17's draw_text did. Now look at the gap between the T and the A. It's wider than the gap between the E and the R, by a distance you can see from across the room, because the T's bar leaves a hole underneath it that the A's slope was designed to slide into. The font knows this. It has a table of pairs that sit badly at their advances and how far to pull each one together, and draw_text never asked. That table is the first thing this chapter reads. The rest of it is the arithmetic of putting glyphs in a row: where the pen goes, where the next line goes, where a line has to stop, and what to do with the space left over when it does. None of it draws a pixel, and that turns out to be the point.

Every previous chapter about type ended with a picture. This one ends with a list of numbers and hands the list to chapter 17. Layout decides where every glyph goes; rendering puts it there. Every serious text stack keeps those two apart, and by the end of the chapter you'll be able to see the seam between them in your own code.

§ 18.1 The pen, and the lines around it

A run of text is a pen walking along a baseline. It starts at some x, draws a glyph with its origin there, moves right by that glyph's advance, and repeats. Advances are in font units, so the step in pixels is advance · size / units_per_em, which chapter 17 already wrapped as pen_advance. layout_run(font, text, size, x, y, kerning) does the walk without drawing anything and answers a list of placements, one per character in order: the glyph's name and the fractional pixel position of its origin. Every character gets a placement, including spaces, which have an advance and nothing else, and characters the font lacks, which place .notdef. run_advance is how far the pen moved in total. The last parameter is the subject of the next section; pass false for now.

The font's vertical numbers become pixels the same way. ascent is how far the font reaches above the baseline, descent how far below (a positive number: the file stores the descender as a negative one), and line_height is the distance from one baseline to the next: ascent plus descent plus the file's line_gap, which for Roboto is zero. At 16 pixels the lines are 18.75 pixels apart, and not a whole number, which matters later.

§ 18.2 Kerning

Back to the T and the A. Roboto's kern table says the pair T A should be pulled together by 79 font units, A V by 87, and, since the order matters, A T by 129. kern(font, left, right) reads the table and answers 0 for a pair it doesn't list, which is nearly all of them: the file has 2,171 pairs out of 177² possible. With kerning on, layout_run moves the pen by the pair value, scaled to pixels, before placing each glyph after the first, and run_advance shrinks by the same amount. That gives the scenario its cleanest line: a kerned pair is narrower than the sum of its advances by exactly the kern. Figure 18.2 sets TAVERN both ways with a tick at every pen position. Two pairs kern, and the whole word comes in five pixels shorter.

§ 18.3 Breaking lines

A paragraph is a run that's too long. The measure is how wide a line may be, and breaking a paragraph into lines that fit it is a problem with a hundred years of literature, most of it about doing better than the obvious thing. This chapter does the obvious thing, because it's what every text field on your screen does: greedy breaking. Take the words one at a time. If the current line with the next word on the end still fits, add it. If not, the line is done and the word starts the next one. break_lines(font, text, size, measure, kerning) answers the lines as strings.

break_lines(font, text, size, measure, kerning):
  words ← the runs of non-space characters in text
  lines ← [];  current ← ""
  for word in words:
    candidate ← current = "" ? word : current + " " + word
    if current ≠ "" and run_advance(font, candidate, size, kerning) > measure:
      append current to lines;  current ← word
    else:
      current ← candidate
  if current ≠ "": append current to lines
  return lines

Three decisions are hidden in those ten lines, and the scenarios pin each of them. A line exactly as wide as the measure fits: the comparison is greater than, and the second scenario puts a line on the boundary and then a hundredth of a pixel past it. A word wider than the measure can't be broken, so it sits alone on its own line and sticks out; the alternative, breaking inside a word, is hyphenation, and it's a chapter in someone else's book. And the width of a candidate line is measured with kerning if the line will be laid out with kerning, so the pair across a space counts (Roboto kerns space T). Runs of spaces collapse to one break, spaces at the ends vanish, and a text with no words has no lines.

§ 18.4 Four alignments

Every line but the last comes out a bit short of the measure, and what you do with the slack, the measure minus the line's width, is the alignment. Leave it on the right and the paragraph is set left, with a ragged right edge. Put it on the left and it's set right. Split it and the line is centered. The fourth choice is the one books use: spread the slack across the spaces, so every gap in the line grows by the same amount and the line ends exactly at the measure. That's justified, and it needs a line with at least one space; a line that's one word can't be stretched and is laid out left instead. layout_line(font, text, size, x, y, measure, align, kerning) is layout_run plus one of those four rules, with align one of the strings "left", "right", "center", "justify".

layout_line(font, text, size, x, y, measure, align, kerning):
  run ← layout_run(font, text, size, x, y, kerning)
  slack ← measure − run_advance(font, text, size, kerning)
  if align = "justify" and text has a space:
    extra ← slack / (number of spaces in text)
    for each placement, add extra × (spaces before it in text) to its x
  else:
    shift ← align = "right" ? slack : align = "center" ? slack / 2 : 0
    add shift to every placement's x
  return run

A paragraph is lines stacked: layout_paragraph breaks the text, lays out each line with the same alignment, puts the first baseline at y and each next one line_height lower, and answers one flat list of placements, since the renderer doesn't care which line a glyph came from. It keeps one rule of its own: a justified paragraph's last line is laid out left, because stretching three words across the whole measure is how you can tell a text engine was written in a weekend.

§ 18.5 The seam

Now draw it. draw_run(canvas, font, run, size, color, linear) takes the placements and hands each one to chapter 17: split its x with subpixel_of into a whole pixel and a quarter, fetch the glyph's bitmap for that quarter, and paint it with the pen at that pixel. The baseline needs a whole pixel row too, and there's no vertical equivalent of the quarters, so it rounds to the nearest row, halves up: at 16 pixels the baselines land on rows 24, 43, 62, 80. That's the seam. Everything above it is arithmetic on a string; everything below it is chapter 17, unchanged. A run laid out at whole pixels draws exactly what draw_text drew, and the scenario says so with a diff of zero.

The trap

The obvious shortcut is to round the pen to a whole pixel after every glyph, so the bitmap for quarter zero is the only one you ever need. Each rounding is off by at most half a pixel, which sounds harmless. It isn't, because the errors don't cancel: an i at 11 pixels advances by 2.67, and rounded it advances by 3, every time, so twenty of them drift six and a half pixels. Figure 18.4 sets a line chosen to be mean about it. The rounded version is fifteen pixels longer, and every word in it is a different amount too wide. Keep the pen fractional and round only when you paint; the quarter-pixel cache in chapter 17 exists for exactly this reason.

§ 18.6 Putting it together

The plate is one paragraph set four ways. Every glyph in it went through layout_paragraph, then draw_run, and the only thing that changes between the panels is a string.
